1 · Sample dispatch — closing the loop

The bridge is deployed, the signed webhook is verified end to end, 73 of 73 delivery addresses are restored, and the website now writes the address on every new submission.

The bug, plainly. pipedrive.js contained zero references to street or postcode. Someone previously hit the same problem for State, fixed that one field, and stopped. Pipedrive hides it by returning success: true while discarding values that have nowhere to land.

Two things left, both small

  1. Verify one live submission end to end.
  2. dispatch.ts must read the Person’s address first — 68 of 86 sample leads have no organisation, because they are homeowners.

Then the carrier comes off test mode and Jess stops re-typing. 12–15 addresses a day, roughly an hour.

Decisions

Confirm what “sent” meansThe Australia Post scan, not the label printing. A printed label sitting on a shelf never shows as sent. That is what is built.
eParcelAlpha qualifies at ~3,000–3,750 parcels a year against a 2,000 threshold. Free, removes the $126/month fee, gives true lodgement confirmation and better rates. Open since 8 September; Jess is the named contact.

2 · The form rebuild

Cleaning 74 addresses showed what people type when a form asks a question with no right answer.

Unit boxStreet boxWhat they meant
99 Sunnymede Lane9 Sunnymede Lane
94Ring Street94 Ring Street
211 Sundew St2/11 Sundew St
1-9, 8Mulligan Stnobody knows

Plus VIC typed into the postcode box, and one person whose fields all shifted one across, so their suburb reads “Street”.

None of this is user error. Unit / House No. is required, and most people do not have one.

The fix — one day

  • One address line instead of two.
  • Postcode first, which auto-fills suburb and state from the free Australia Post dataset.
  • A “Sending to:” confirmation line before submit.

That removes four of the five defect classes at source, and the parser stops being the only defence. The 2 September spec already specified one address line — the build diverged. This finishes something already decided.

It also unblocks three other sprints: Australia Post needs clean addresses, cabinet makers naming their stonemasons grows the relationship data on its own, and architects self-identify for CPD.

4 · Training — Sprint H is at 0%

The build is 85% ahead of adoption.

Open, never closed485 — 386 are meetings, oldest due 484 days
Showroom visits carrying a company0%
Leads still sitting on “New”93 of 101

This is not an effort problem and should not be presented as one. 3,973 activities in six months is a team that is working. 6% person-attachment is too uniform across meetings and calls to be individual habit — it is what happens when activity is logged from the organisation record or straight off the calendar.

Three things, trainable in twenty minutes

  1. Every activity carries a company and a person.
  2. Tick it done, and pick the right type — formal meeting or sample drop.
  3. Never leave a visit without booking the next one.

Do the machine’s share first — backfill the person where a company has exactly one contact, and fix the showroom check-in in code. Nobody should be asked to hand-fix what a script can.

Another week of building changes none of these numbers. One week of logging three things properly changes all of them.
