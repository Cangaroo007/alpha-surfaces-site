2 · Monthly forecast — a tour

Back to agenda

Each salesperson forecasts the slabs they expect each key account to order next month, and submits by the 1st. Reminders come every weekday until they do. During the month, actual vs forecast shows how it is tracking for the team, each salesperson and each client.

Monthly forecast
One box per key account, with the last three months, the 3-month average, last year and committed beside it. Saves as you type. Works on a phone.

Actual vs forecast

Open the settings
WhenWhat happens
From the 20thThe page moves on to next month
From the 25thEach salesperson who hasn’t submitted gets a short email at 7:30am every weekday: how many key accounts are done, which are left, one button. Belinda and Sean get who has and hasn’t

FridaysEach salesperson gets how their month is tracking, the clients behind, and a nudge to update anything that has changed. The first number submitted is kept, so accuracy can be judged on both

Key accounts are the national top 50 on the last 12 months’ sales plus anything in the Spotlight, each under its Pipedrive owner. Other customers who ordered in the last six months are listed below them as optional. Actual is slabs ordered, the same as the Top 50’s MTD figure.

Nothing has been through it yet. November is the first month; reminders would start on Monday 26 October, and the strategy day on 29–30 October is the natural place for everyone to enter it together.

To decide: is the top 50 the right set, or should each salesperson forecast every account that ordered in the last six months? And: some key accounts, Accent Benchtops among them, are still owned in Pipedrive by people who have left. Nobody is reminded about those until they are reassigned.

Column on Top 50What it means
CommittedSlabs on sales orders that have been entered in Datapel but not invoiced yet: ready to process, picked or packed, or waiting for stock
Back ordersThe part of committed that Datapel marks “no stock available”. It is inside committed, not on top: North Coast’s 751 committed included the 54 on back order

Worth a look in Datapel: 78 orders across 13 customers have sat at “picked” for more than 60 days, some since January. Either the stock is being held for the customer or the orders were never closed off. Closing them off makes committed mean stock that is really waiting to go out.

5 · Duplicate companies — the plan

Back to agenda

146 groups of companies in Pipedrive look like the same business. Not all of them are: Classic Pools in Loganlea and Classic Pools in Hoppers Crossing are two branches. Each group has been sorted, with a suggested record to keep. Nothing has been merged: a merge in Pipedrive can’t be undone.

SuggestionGroupsWhy
Merge94Same name and the same suburb, or only one of them has an address. 99 records would go
Check43Same name, same state, different suburbs — often separate branches (Builders Discount Warehouse in Brendale, Slacks Creek and Arundel)
Keep separate9Same name in different states

How the record to keep is chosen: the one linked to a Datapel customer, then won deals, then the most activities and people, then the one with an address, then the oldest. When merged, everything on the other record (people, activities, deals, notes) moves onto the one kept.

To decide: in 28 of the merges the two records have different owners, so someone has to say who keeps the company. The proposal is one sitting of Belinda and Olivia with the list: approve the 94, settle the 28 owners, and look at the 43. The merges are then run from the Company clean-up page.

7 · A campaign builder in RoadRunner

Back to agenda

Every campaign so far has been built by Sean. Stacey asked on Monday to run one for her region. A builder page in RoadRunner is now built: put a campaign landing page together (the stones, the offer, the FAQs, the showrooms) with a chat assistant beside it, send it for testing, and have it approved to go live. Sean is testing it first; nobody else has it yet.

StepWhat happens
BuildFill in the page or ask the assistant (“a Sunshine Coast version of free samples for builders, leading with Alpha Zero”). The preview is the real page, in Alpha’s layout and colours, with the usual sample form
TestA private link to share, test emails to yourself, and automatic checks: links, stones, forms, tracking
LiveApproved with one click. The audience is checked against Pipedrive before any email goes. The previous version is kept, so going back is one click too

The idea that makes it safe: a campaign becomes content in RoadRunner, not new code on the website. The assistant can change words, pictures, stones and FAQs; it can’t publish, send or change the website itself. Something the template can’t do yet becomes a request to Sean.

Not in it yet: sending the campaign email. That comes next, through the same Pipedrive check every send already goes through.

Four questions: who approves a campaign going live; is Stacey’s Sunshine Coast campaign the pilot; should campaign emails come from the rep’s own address or from hello@; and does it start before or after the 29–30 October training?
