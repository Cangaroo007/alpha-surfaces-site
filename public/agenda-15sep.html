1 · Training — built and live

Back to agenda

The whole programme is now a page, in Alpha branding, with live screenshots from our own CRM. It opens with the decisions, not the content.

Pipedrive — The Five Habits
The training page. Nine questions, then the habits, each shown on a real Pipedrive screen.
Open the page

The three non-negotiables became the Five Habits — habits to build rather than rules to break.

  1. Company is the parent. Person is the child.
  2. Every person carries a title.
  3. Every activity carries a company and a person — start from the contact record, not the company record.
  4. Tick it done, and pick the right type.
  5. Never leave a visit without booking the next one.

Habit 3 is the one that matters, and it isn’t about effort.

Open the person and log the activity from there, and Pipedrive fills the company in for you. Start from the company and it never asks you for a person — which is exactly how we got to 7%.

The nine questions are on the page, grouped by who owns them. In short: Belinda has five, Pam has three, and I have one — run the scripts before session 1 so nobody is asked to hand-fix what a script can.

Built to Pam’s five beats, in her order: congratulate them, introduce the brand, show the collections, introduce Pam as their dedicated contact, and close by proposing an introduction rather than a sample drop. The note changes register with the size of their night, so a single-category winner is never told their night was “a system”.

They are 100% regional. Across the original 53 — Far North Queensland 13 · Downs & Western 13 · North Queensland 9 · Wide Bay Burnett 9 · Mackay & Whitsunday 5 · Central Queensland 4. Zero from Brisbane, Gold Coast or Sunshine Coast — which has consequences for stock, delivery and which rep follows up.

Two of them are already Pam’s accounts — Taylor Builders and Neater Constructions. A cold “we may not have met yet” to an account she owns reads badly, so both pages now open with a re-connect line instead. Ellis Developments falls out on its own — non-benchtop, and hers.

Two things worth knowing before anything sends. Triumph Enterprises now trades as ARW Homes — address them as ARW, or the email reads off a stale list. And G.J Gardner Mackay and Hotondo Toowoomba are franchise siblings of accounts Stacey already works — worth a word with her first.

The email addresses are no longer the blocker.

All 48 were sourced from public sources on Monday — 32 verified off the company’s own website, 6 inferred from a confirmed domain, and 10 not found. None was invented: a guessed address that hard-bounces costs more than a missing one, because it damages the sending domain for everyone else on the list. Everything else stays guarded — 144 placeholders across the 48 pages, and the send script physically refuses any message still containing one. Nothing has been sent.

Worth naming, because it cost six days: the wait was never Pam. The send list was created on 9 September at 21:50 and its modified time is still identical to its created time — it has never been opened. Her 8 September email said exactly that: she was being asked to request permission to see it. Sharing it on screen in this meeting is the whole fix.

One timing question worth deciding together: Queensland state winners are announced in October, drawn from these same regional winners. So there is a natural choice — send now, or hold and make it one bigger run alongside the state announcement. Either works; it is worth picking on purpose rather than by default.

Decision one — create Sample drop

  • Two-minute settings change. Two custom types already exist, so there is no plan limit in the way.
  • ⚠️ The trap. The coverage report counts contact as type in call, meeting, lunch, showroom visit. Add the type without adding it to that list and every sample drop silently stops counting — coverage gets worse overnight for a reason nobody connects back to a settings change made a fortnight earlier. Both change in the same sitting.

Decision two — the six roles

  • Owner / Director · Manager · Estimator · Designer / Specifier · Sales / Showroom · Admin / Accounts.
  • A new single-select field, so it can be filtered. Labels can’t be, because a person can be Estimator and Cabinetmaker at the same time. Homeowners don’t need one.
  • Already solved, worth knowing: business type has a proper home — Business Category, populated on ~82% of people. The script that fills it from the company record already exists and ran on 24 August, stamping 1,337 contacts. Worth re-running for the ~117 contacts added since.

4 · Sample dispatch — closing the loop

Back to agenda

73 of 73. Every sample request in Pipedrive now carries a complete, structured delivery address — and every new one has since Monday.

Nobody did anything wrong here. The addresses were never missing. The form collected them and the website database stored them — they simply never reached the CRM, because the code that writes to Pipedrive had no reference to street or postcode. Pipedrive returns success: true while discarding values with nowhere to land, so every layer reported that it had worked.

And we had never shown Jess how to run dispatch through Pipedrive, so there was no reason for anyone to go looking. That part is training, and it is session 2.

Two things left, both small: verify one live submission end to end, and make dispatch.ts read the Person’s address first — 68 of 86 sample leads have no organisation, because they’re homeowners. Then the carrier flips off test mode and Jess stops re-typing 12–15 addresses a day.

Decisions

  • Confirm “sent” means the Australia Post scan, not the label printing. A printed label on a shelf never shows as sent. That’s what’s built.
  • eParcel — Alpha qualifies at ~3,000–3,750 parcels a year against a 2,000 threshold. Free, removes the $126/month Starshipit fee, gives true lodgement confirmation and better rates. Open since 8 September; Jess is the named contact.
