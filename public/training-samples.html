Sample requests — how to process them

Pipedrive, step by step

For Jess  —  written up from our call, Tuesday 15 September

The short version

Nothing you are doing today is wrong. This just moves where you read the request from — out of the email, into Pipedrive — so that the next step can do the typing for you.

This week

Open the request in Pipedrive instead of working off the email. Copy the address straight from the lead into MyPost Business. Set the stage, and fill in Samples sent with the date you posted it. File the email as you always have — it is still your backup.

Once the wiring is finished

One button. The label prints with the address already on it, the tracking number writes itself onto the lead, and Samples sent fills itself in the moment Australia Post scans the parcel. You stop typing addresses altogether.

You asked whether it might not go perfectly at first. It might not — and that is fine. Everything below is reversible, nothing here can send a parcel on its own, and if something looks wrong the right move is to stop and message Sean rather than work around it.

1 · Open the Leads Inbox

Back to the top

Pipedrive → Leads → Leads Inbox. Every website sample request lands here by itself, titled Sample Request — name.

Click the Lead created column heading twice so the arrow points down — newest at the top. That is the order you want to work in. Every row is labelled HOT, the source is API (meaning it came from the website, not typed by hand), and they are all owned by you.

There are 106 in there. Most are old and already dealt with — you are not expected to go back through them. Work the new ones from the top, and we will sort the history out separately.

2 · Open it and read the request

Back to the top

Click the row. Everything the person told us is on this one screen.

The left column is the fields, the yellow box on the right is the note. The note is the request exactly as it came off the website — the SR number, the stones, what they said, and whether they ticked marketing consent.

“Stonemason (company)” does not mean the company is a stonemason.

The question on the website is “who is making your benchtop?”, and the list they pick from is every company we have — because a homeowner usually knows their kitchen company, not the stonemason who actually cuts the stone. So a cabinet maker like Lambert Kitchens showing up in a field called Stonemason (company) is the system working, not a mistake. The field is badly named and we are renaming it. Until then, read it as “who they named”.

3 · The address is on the lead — scroll down

Back to the top

You do not need the email for the address. Scroll down the same panel, past the fields, to PERSON.

Delivery address, in full, on the lead. Email and phone are blurred here for the screenshot only — you will see them. Copy the address from here straight into MyPost Business.

The two-window trick. Keep Pipedrive open in one window and MyPost Business in another, side by side, and read across. Australia Post starts suggesting the address about halfway through typing it, so you rarely type the whole thing. That alone should save you the pen and paper step.

If the address looks wrong — a unit number repeated, a state in the postcode box, a suburb that is obviously a street — fix it in Australia Post, send the parcel, and tell Sean which one it was. Those are form problems, not your problems, and each one you report gets the form fixed. Do not guess at an address; a parcel to the wrong house costs more than a day's delay.

4 · Mark it, so everyone else can see where it is up to

Back to the top

Two things, both on the lead. Hover the field and a small pencil appears.

Lead stage. Five options. You only need the first three.

WhenSet it to
You have picked it up and you are collecting the samplesWorking
It is in the post — also put that date in Samples sentFollowing up
Nothing to do — a test, a duplicate, or not a real requestClosed, and add a Lead outcome

A correction to what I told you on the call. I said “Working means the sample has been sent”. That was wrong, and it is worth getting right now rather than unpicking later. Working means you are on it. The thing that records that a sample actually went is the Samples sent date — and that is the field Australia Post will fill in by itself once the wiring is done. If Working also meant “sent”, there would be no stage that means “Jess has this, it is in progress”, which is exactly what the rest of the team needs to see.

It is one extra click, at the same moment you were already in the record.

5 · When they name a company, tell Belinda

Back to the top

This is the part that turns a sample request into a sales lead, and right now it only happens if you do it.

If Stonemason (company) has a name in it — a kitchen company, a cabinet maker, a stonemason, anyone — send Belinda a short email: who asked for samples, what they are building, and who they named. That is a reason for a rep to ring that company, which is the whole point of asking the question.

If the note says “Stonemason: NONE — asked to be connected with one”, that person has actively asked us to recommend someone. Those are the best ones. Same email to Belinda, flagged as needing a referral, so a rep can put a local stonemason in front of them.

Worth knowing

That answer is captured, but at the moment it only appears as a line in the note — there is no field for it, so it cannot be filtered or reported on yet. That is on Sean to fix. Until then, read the note.

Not yet

Don't try to work out which stonemason to recommend yourself, and don't reply to the customer with a name. Territory and who-gets-what is Belinda's call, and she has the context you would be guessing at.

The questions you asked

Back to the top

Can I still choose Express instead of Standard?

  • Today, yes — you are in the Australia Post portal, so nothing has changed. Tick it as you always have.
  • When the automation lands, not yet. Checked the code: the service is set once for the whole system, not per parcel, so the first version will send everything Standard. That is being fixed before it goes live, because one a week is a real need and a sales rep asking for tomorrow is exactly when it matters.
  • Until it is fixed, the fallback is the one you already know: do that one in the portal by hand. Tell Sean if it is more than one or two a week — that changes how urgent it is.

Will the label still print on our printer?

  • Yes. Nothing changes about the printer, the label, or the walk up the hill. The only thing that changes is that the address is already filled in.

What is eParcel, and do I need to do anything?

  • It is Australia Post's business account — better rates, proper lodgement confirmation, and it lets us connect to Australia Post directly instead of through a middleman we currently pay $126 a month for.
  • On the volume you are doing, Alpha qualifies — roughly 3,000–3,750 parcels a year against a 2,000 threshold.
  • Sean is sending you the exact steps separately. It is an enquiry form in the Australia Post portal and then they call you. When they do, ask about pickups — worst case they say no.

Is the payment still the saved card?

  • Yes — the same card on the MyPost Business account. Nothing new to set up, and no charge happens until a label is actually bought.

Two things to watch for

Back to the top

There is a test request in the list from Sean. Sample Request — Sean Stone, 13 September, a New South Wales address. It is real-looking and it is not real. Do not post it. It is being removed — if it is still there when you read this, set it to Closed and leave it.

Old requests look different from new ones. Anything from before 13 September was created before the address fix, so the fields may be half empty. Work from the note on those, not the fields. New ones are complete.
