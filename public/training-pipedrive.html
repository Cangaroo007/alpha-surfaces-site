Why we’re doing this

The team logged 3,973 completed activities in six months — roughly sixteen a day across the group. 199 of them are complete records.

That is not an effort problem and it should not be presented as one. Habit one

Company is the parent. Person is the child.

Back to contents

Every person belongs to a company — except a homeowner, who stands on their own.

The company tells us the account was covered. The person tells us who we actually know inside it. People are the only thing our marketing can reach, so a company with no named contact is a company we can never send anything to.

Do

Search for the company first. Link the person to it. If the company genuinely isn’t in Pipedrive, flag it to Pam or Jess so it gets created properly — once, with a category and an owner.

Don’t

Create an organisation from the lead or person screen. The old auto–create path made 152 junk organisations between May and August before it was removed. Two spellings of the same stonemason is worse than none.

One thing that catches everyone: changing a company’s owner does not move its people. They stay behind with the old rep. That is why 1,062 contacts currently sit with someone other than whoever owns their company. A script clears the backlog; the habit stops it coming back.

Habit two

Every person carries a title.

Back to contents

Even a guessed one. A contact with no title is a name we can’t route, can’t segment, and can’t brief a rep on before a visit.

The live People list. The Labels column is empty all the way down. And look at the names — Sales– Keys & Co Kitchens, Sales– Pool Systems, Admin– Airlie Tiles. People have been typing the job into the name field, because there has never been anywhere else to put it. That is the whole argument for this habit, and the team made it themselves.

Where it goes today: nowhere. Checked live against the account — the Person record has eleven custom fields and not one of them is a job title or a role. The only place a title exists is inside Label, mixed in with business types. Of the 23 Label options, exactly five are job titles: CEO, Director, Sales Rep, Estimator and Client Liason (spelled like that). The rest are trades and pool tags.

So this needs one new field — Role, single–select, six values. Single–select rather than free text because “National Specification Manager” is lovely and completely unfilterable. And a new field rather than reusing Label, which is already the reps’ working tag on 2,946 records and got into its current state by being asked to do two jobs at once.

RoleCoversWhy it earns a slot
Owner / DirectorOwner, director, principal, partner, CEO Decides. Pricing, supply, display commitments
ManagerGM, operations, production, sales manager, office manager Runs it day to day, and decides in practice
EstimatorEstimator, quoting, takeoff Chooses the stone at the quote. The contact we most under–record
Designer / SpecifierArchitect, interior designer, kitchen designer Puts our colour on the drawing. The CPD audience
Sales / ShowroomSales rep, consultant, front of house Recommends our colour — or somebody else’s
Admin / AccountsAdministration, accounts, scheduler, reception Orders, invoices, deliveries — and the delivery address

Homeowners don’t need one. They’re already identified by having no company, plus the Consumer label. And blank is allowed — a person with no role shows up on a “needs a role” list, which is more useful than an Unknown option that quietly becomes everyone’s default.

Nobody types 2,018 roles. A script reads the job–title half of the existing labels first and fills in what it can. The website forms capture it at source from then on. Reps only set it on the contacts they actually meet, as they meet them.

Habit three

Every activity carries a company and a person.

Back to contents

No company and it didn’t happen. No person and we can never contact them again.

Start from the contact record, not the company record.

That is the entire fix. Open the person, log the activity from there, and Pipedrive fills the company in for you. Start from the company and it never asks you for a person — which is exactly how we got to 7%.

Step one — open the person, not the company. A real stonemason contact. Business Category is set to Stonemason, so the trade is right. But Add labels is empty, there is no role field anywhere in the Details panel, and Activities (0) — not one logged interaction, ever. Then click Schedule an activity.

Step two — and here is the payoff. Because we started from Glen’s record, both boxes at the bottom are already filled in: the person Glen Fail, and the organisation Sunshine Coast Stone Pty Ltd. Nobody typed either one. Pick the type along the top, set the time, tick Mark as done if it has already happened, save. That is a complete record, and it took no extra effort at all.

Do

Start from the contact. If more than one person was in the room, add them all — Pipedrive allows it and it’s the truest record of the visit.

Don’t

Log it from the organisation record, or let it appear from your calendar and leave it. Both produce an activity with a company, no person, and no way to follow up with anyone.

Habit four

Tick it done, and pick the right type.

Back to contents

485 activities are open and never closed. 386 of them are meetings. The oldest was due 484 days ago.

An unticked meeting is wrong in both directions — it never counts as coverage, and it sits on your to–do list forever. Ticking it is one click and it is the difference between a record and a reminder.

These are all eight types on the account. Call, Meeting, Task, Deadline, Email, Lunch, Personal Leave, Showroom Visit. There is no sample drop — so today a two–minute drop–off at the counter and a booked hour with a decision maker are recorded as exactly the same thing.

That is why neither number can be trusted. A sample drop is real coverage and it resets the clock on that account — but it is not one of the six booked appointments a day. Two different denominators, and right now they are the same bucket.

Posted samples create no activity at all. Posting is not a visit.

One warning for whoever builds it. The coverage report counts contact as done and type in call, meeting, lunch, showroom visit. Add Sample drop without adding it to that list too and every sample drop silently stops counting — coverage gets worse overnight for a reason nobody will connect back to a settings change made a fortnight earlier. Both change in the same sitting.

Habit five

Never leave a visit without booking the next one.

Back to contents

This is what turns the CRM from a logbook into a call cycle.

2,085 of 3,025 organisations have no record of contact at all. Some of that is missing records, some is deliberate, and some is genuine neglect — and right now there is no way to tell which. One booked next step on every visit fixes that permanently, because an account with nothing scheduled becomes visible immediately.

Do it before you leave the carpark, on your phone, even if it is a placeholder six weeks out. A date you move later is infinitely better than no date. When you tick an activity done, Pipedrive offers to schedule the next one in the same breath — that is the moment, and it takes about four seconds.

An account with nothing booked is an account nobody is coming back to.

That is the only thing the “needs a visit” list is looking for, and it is the list that will drive the six–week call cycles and the trip planner.

Leads — the stage is how everyone else knows whose turn it is

Back to contents

The live Leads inbox, pulled today. 102 leads. Read the Next activity column — No activity, every single row, all the way down. Every one is owned by Jess and has never been passed to a rep. Every one is tagged HOT. The oldest on this screen came in on 31 May.

These are people who filled in a form on our website asking for our product. They are the warmest thing we have and they are sitting untouched, in some cases for three and a half months.

The stages were rebuilt on 6 September, but the rename was the small part. The point is that moving the stage is part of the job — it is the only way anyone else can tell whose turn it is.

StageWhose turn
NewUs — arrived, untouched
WorkingUs — we owe them something
Following upThem — delivered, automation running
QualifiedThe rep — a real opportunity
ClosedNobody — and it must carry an outcome

Closed without an outcome is the worst record in the system, because “they bought” and “they ghosted” look identical and conversion becomes unreportable.

And one that is easy to miss: Referred to stonemason is the win for an influencer lead. A homeowner enquiry has no won state of its own — the revenue event is their stonemason buying the slab. Which is why the one question on every influencer is always “who’s your stonemason?”
